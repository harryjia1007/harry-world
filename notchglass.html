converted on your Mac, not on someone's server.

Common formats use what's already built into macOS. Some video and audio formats need FFmpeg, which you install separately. The new file shows up next to the original with its own name, so nothing gets overwritten.

when music plays, the notch shows it.

A small cover on one side of the notch, an animated playback indicator on the other. Move the pointer up and the full player opens: timeline, play and pause, previous and next.

Artwork, playback controls and seeking depend on the player, its version and the permissions you allow. The clip is an interface demo, not a compatibility test.

There's a cup icon too. Click it and the display won't sleep from inactivity until you click again or quit. You can still put the Mac to sleep or close the lid.

NotchGlass Help

Do I need a MacBook with a notch?

No. On Macs without one, it sits at the top center of the screen and works the same way.

Does anything I convert get uploaded?

No. Conversions run on your Mac. The app goes online only to check your license, look for updates and look up artwork for the song that's playing. Files you drop on iCloud Drive are copied to your iCloud folder, and macOS syncs them.

How do I install and activate it?

Download the app from your Gumroad receipt and move it to Applications. Open Settings › License, paste the license key from your purchase and click Activate. The app is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

How do updates work?

Download the latest release from your Gumroad purchase receipt. Quit NotchGlass and replace the app in Applications. Settings and the license stored in Keychain are preserved. Updates are installed manually.

Why does it ask about media access?

To show what's playing and control the player, macOS asks whether NotchGlass may access media information. You can change this later in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation.

Where do converted files go?

Next to the original, with a new name. Nothing is overwritten.
